Anyone who grew up with Indonesian internet cafés knows the unwritten rule:
don't download big files. It drags the whole warnet's bandwidth down, and if
the operator notices, you're out. WarNet asks what would happen if you broke
that rule on purpose, and did everything you could to get away with it.

## The idea

GameSeed gave us two themes: "So Absurd, It's Cool" and "Childhood Dreams".
Back when warnets were everywhere, I always wondered what it would be like to
actually start that huge download and then do whatever it took not to get
caught. That was the childhood dream. Turning it into a stealth game, with
the operator as the threat and your own PC as the hiding place, took care of
the absurd.

## How a session plays

You sit at one PC and can look in four directions: your screen, your left,
your back, and your right. Open the browser and the download starts. From
then on, the game is about hiding what you're doing until it finishes.

## Three pillars

My job as game designer was to break the idea down into a few systems the
team could build. "Don't get caught while downloading" became three pillars,
each one an obstacle or a way around it:

<Points>
  <Point title="Random events">Things happen around you, often with the people nearby, and you have to deal with them.</Point>
  <Point title="Side tasks">Cover for the download: chatting, playing games, refreshing pages, looking busy.</Point>
  <Point title="Abilities">Tools like hacking that buy you room when suspicion climbs.</Point>
</Points>

The most absurd moment comes when they all collide: three random events at
once, the operator almost sure of you, and you in the middle of a hack.

## Design into code

The hardest part wasn't any single system. It was getting the design out of
my head and the document, and into the code, with a team of eight. It's hard
to make sure everyone has read the design document, let alone understood it
the same way. A lot of my month went into explaining the design to the team
while we built it.

> Small scope first. Get the core loop and mechanics working, then expand and polish.

## What we cut

Time ran out before the list did. We cut a lot of random events and side
tasks, and I wish we'd had time to polish the game. That's what WarNet taught
me: keep the scope small, make the core loop work first, and only then add
more.
