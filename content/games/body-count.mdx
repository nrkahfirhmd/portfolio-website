Body Count drops you on a rooftop over Shibuya's rush-hour crossing with a
target list and a limited number of catches. Somewhere in the crowd below is
the one person who matches every trait. Find them before the wave runs out.

## Reading the theme

The jam theme was **Compact**. We read it two ways. First, the crowd itself:
thousands of people with different looks, all moving through one small
crossing, squeezed onto a single screen so that finding one person becomes a
test of attention. Second, the controls: one tap makes a catch, and there's
nothing else to press.

## The core loop

<Points>
  <Point title="Read. Spot. Tap.">Check the target's hair, shirt, pants, and bag, find someone who matches all four, and tap to catch them. "ANY" accepts any colour or bag; "X" means no bag at all.</Point>
  <Point title="Clear. Upgrade. Repeat.">Hit the wave's score target before your catches run out, pick an upgrade, and face a bigger crowd.</Point>
</Points>

Catches are the constraint. Upgrades and special passersby are how you buy
some of that room back, so a good run is as much about spending them well as
about sharp eyes.

## A crowd that stays light

A crossing full of people sounds expensive, especially on an iPhone. The
trick is that the game never draws hundreds of people at once. Each
pedestrian gets their own speed and their own moment to cross, new ones spawn
only when the crowd needs them, and anyone who walks past the bottom edge is
removed. The crossing always looks packed, but only the people on screen
exist.

## Tuning the difficulty

Each wave starts the crowd a little bigger than the last. I tuned the
difficulty mostly around each wave's score target and the abilities the
player had picked up by then.

Playtesting turned up a different problem: a lot of players were frustrated
by the target prompt, the list of traits to match. It was a sentence to read
("Brown Shirt, White Hat, with White Bag") while the crowd kept moving. We
redesigned it as four slots, hair, shirt, pants, and bag, that you can take
in at a glance.

## What I'd change

The idea is for everything to point your attention back at the crowd:
clothing colours are the puzzle, the moving pedestrians are the pressure. We
didn't get all the way there. If I made it again, I'd work on cohesion and
the core loop first, because those are where it fell short.
