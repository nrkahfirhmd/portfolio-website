A warung owner restocks by gut feeling: how much rice, how many eggs, how
much instant noodles for tomorrow. Guess too low and customers walk to the
next shop; guess too high and money sits on the shelf. Sobat Warung is a
WhatsApp assistant that does the guessing with data instead, built by Tim
CEBEES for the AI Innovation Challenge at COMPFEST 2025.

## Why WhatsApp

Warung owners already live in WhatsApp; it's how they talk to suppliers,
family, and customers every day. So there's no app to install and nothing
new to learn. They register their shop and log the day's sales just by
chatting, and the forecasts come back in the same chat.

## What to buy tomorrow

The forecast answers one question: how many of each product should the owner
buy for tomorrow. It's a lag-based ensemble: the past sales of every product
go through Darts, which forecasts many time series at once with a single
global model, into three LightGBM models whose predictions are averaged.

The models also see what's coming, not just what happened: holidays, the day
of the week, and fuel prices go in as future covariates. They're evaluated
on RMSLE, which treats best-sellers and slow movers fairly and punishes
under-prediction hard. For a warung, running out costs more than a little
extra stock.

## What to bundle

Slow-selling products tie up money. To move them, Sobat Warung suggests
bundles, found with FP-Growth: it compresses the shop's transactions into a
compact tree of patterns, then reads off which products customers buy
together.

## My role

I was the backend developer and machine learning engineer on a team of
three. The backend is FastAPI with MongoDB, with Twilio carrying the
WhatsApp conversation.

## Where it stands

We didn't make the finals. Sobat Warung is a proof of concept: it hasn't
been tried by real warung owners yet. The next step would be exactly that, putting it in front of a
few shops and seeing whether the forecasts beat their instinct.
